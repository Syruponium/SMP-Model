# COSMOGENESIS: Star Formation via Matrix Compression

## The Hypothesis
Standard astrophysics struggles to explain why gas clouds collapse at the exact rates observed. The SMP model proposes that star formation is triggered by a "Mechanical Compression" at the 13.84 K phase boundary ($R_{snap}$).

## The Mechanism
1. **The Viscosity Wall**: As galactic gas rotates toward the 13.84 K threshold, it encounters a sudden increase in the Matrix's viscosity.
2. **Pressure Shock**: This transition creates a localized pressure spike ($P_{comp}$), forcing hydrogen atoms closer together than gravity alone could achieve.
3. **Ignition**: This mechanical "shove" pushes gas clouds past the Jeans Mass limit, igniting star formation.

## Observed Evidence
- **Galactic Rings**: Concentric star-forming regions align with the predicted $R_{snap}$ transition zones.
- **LSB Stability**: In Low Surface Brightness galaxies like DDO 154, the high HI gas content acts as a thermal buffer, smoothing the compression and leading to slower, more sustained star formation.
